# Health Systems Architecture

## North star

Create the conditions for high energy, metabolic resilience, strong cognition, healthy aging, physical capability, psychological stability, and disease resistance—while minimizing unnecessary exposure, treatment burden, and dependency.

The OS is organized by leverage rather than by product category.

## Tier 0 — safety and diagnostic clarity

Before optimization, identify conditions where delay carries meaningful risk.

- Rapid unexplained weight loss
- Persistent blood in stool or urine
- Severe or progressive pain
- Neurological deficits
- Persistent fever
- Breathing difficulty
- Severe dehydration
- New mass or rapidly changing lesion
- Significant anemia or organ dysfunction
- Suicidal thinking or acute psychiatric instability

This tier is not “bureaucratic medicine.” It is recognition that time-sensitive failure modes require fast diagnosis and stabilization before long-horizon root-cause work.

## Tier 1 — the biological foundations

### Sleep and circadian rhythm

**Goal:** reliable recovery, endocrine timing, immune regulation, cognition, and appetite control.

Core variables:

- sleep duration and regularity;
- morning daylight exposure;
- evening light and stimulation;
- room temperature, darkness, and noise;
- caffeine and alcohol timing;
- sleep apnea, nasal obstruction, restless legs, pain, and nocturia.

### Nutrition and nutrient sufficiency

**Goal:** provide adequate energy, protein, essential fats, vitamins, minerals, and phytonutrients without chronic metabolic overload.

Core variables:

- protein adequacy and distribution;
- whole-food quality;
- micronutrient sufficiency;
- fiber and fermentable-food tolerance;
- refined carbohydrate and ultra-processed-food load;
- individualized reactions to dairy, whey, gluten, alcohol, additives, or specific foods;
- food preparation, contamination, and storage quality.

### Movement and physical capacity

**Goal:** preserve strength, muscle, aerobic capacity, mobility, bone, insulin sensitivity, and independence.

Core variables:

- progressive resistance training;
- daily walking and low-intensity movement;
- aerobic base;
- periodic high-intensity work when appropriate;
- mobility and joint-specific rehabilitation;
- avoiding chronic overtraining and under-recovery.

### Nervous-system regulation

**Goal:** reduce chronic sympathetic activation while preserving adaptive challenge.

Core variables:

- workload and uncertainty;
- breathwork, prayer, meditation, and nature;
- social safety and belonging;
- unresolved trauma or chronic conflict;
- digital overstimulation;
- meaningful work, service, and purpose.

## Tier 2 — metabolic and ecological integrity

### Metabolic health

Track the system rather than one number:

- waist and body composition;
- fasting and post-meal glucose where useful;
- triglycerides, HDL, blood pressure, and liver markers;
- energy, hunger, sleep, and exercise recovery;
- muscle mass and strength.

Primary levers:

- resistance training;
- walking after meals;
- whole-food diet;
- adequate sleep;
- sustainable body composition;
- meal timing and fasting used contextually rather than ideologically.

### Gut and digestive ecology

Map:

- chewing and oral health;
- stomach acid and upper-GI symptoms;
- bile and pancreatic function where indicated;
- bowel frequency and stool characteristics;
- food intolerances versus immune or microbial hypotheses;
- inflammatory bowel disease, celiac disease, infection, and malabsorption red flags;
- microbiome-supportive food diversity as tolerated.

### Oral health

Treat the mouth as part of systemic health:

- brushing and interdental cleaning;
- periodontal assessment;
- diet frequency and sugar exposure;
- dry mouth and mouth breathing;
- sleep-disordered breathing;
- unresolved infection or structural problems.

### Infection and parasites

Use exposure-based reasoning:

- country and childhood residence;
- travel and sanitation history;
- untreated water or freshwater exposure;
- barefoot soil exposure in endemic climates;
- undercooked pork, beef, fish, crab, or wild game;
- household outbreaks;
- immunosuppression or planned corticosteroid use;
- unexplained eosinophilia, anemia, diarrhea, malabsorption, rash, or weight loss.

Do not use “parasite” as one undifferentiated diagnosis. Protozoa, roundworms, flukes, tapeworms, and tissue parasites require different tests and treatments.

## Tier 3 — exposure and environment

### Household and personal-care load

Prioritize meaningful exposures over purity theater:

- smoking and secondhand smoke;
- mold and dampness;
- occupational solvents, dusts, fumes, pesticides, and metals;
- indoor air quality and ventilation;
- drinking-water quality;
- cookware and food-storage practices;
- fragranced or irritating products;
- harsh soaps, detergents, and unnecessary antimicrobial use.

Use the hierarchy:

1. Remove high-dose and high-certainty hazards.
2. Reduce chronic exposures with plausible cumulative effects.
3. Avoid spending disproportionate resources on trace exposures while ignoring sleep, diet, smoke, or occupational risk.

### Sun, temperature, and nature

Balance benefits and harms:

- daylight and circadian entrainment;
- outdoor movement and psychological restoration;
- avoiding burns and chronic high-dose UV;
- shade, clothing, and hats before relying exclusively on topical products;
- heat and cold exposure as optional hormetic tools, not substitutes for fundamentals.

## Tier 4 — targeted optimization

### Skin and hair

Sequence:

1. Identify whether the problem is barrier damage, acne, pigmentation, inflammation, infection, hormonal patterning, nutrient deficiency, or structural volume change.
2. Remove harsh cleansers, irritants, friction, and over-treatment.
3. Stabilize sleep, nutrition, stress, and weight.
4. Add the minimum effective topical intervention.
5. Track with standardized photos.

### Supplements

Use when one of four conditions is met:

- documented deficiency;
- high-probability insufficiency;
- strong condition-specific evidence;
- bounded experiment with measurable outcome and low downside.

Avoid supplement accumulation without stop rules.

### Fasting

Treat fasting as a metabolic stressor and tool, not a universal cleanse.

Potential use cases:

- appetite and meal-structure simplification;
- insulin and glucose management in suitable individuals;
- religious or spiritual practice;
- research-informed metabolic experimentation.

Guardrails:

- not automatically appropriate during pregnancy, eating disorders, frailty, underweight, cancer cachexia, uncontrolled diabetes, or medication regimens that can cause hypoglycemia;
- autophagy is tissue- and context-dependent, not a universal clock that begins at one exact hour;
- extended fasts require attention to hydration, electrolytes, recovery, and lean-mass preservation.

## Tier 5 — advanced and clinical interventions

This includes pharmaceuticals, procedures, surgery, hormone therapy, antimicrobial or antiparasitic treatment, and experimental protocols.

Evaluate using:

- severity and natural history of the untreated problem;
- probability and magnitude of benefit;
- absolute rather than only relative effects;
- short- and long-term harms;
- reversibility;
- alternatives and adjuncts;
- interaction with nutrition, sleep, movement, and metabolic health;
- consequences of delay.

## Personal operating loop

### Weekly

- sleep average and consistency;
- strength and movement volume;
- energy, mood, digestion, and symptoms;
- weight/waist only if useful rather than obsessive;
- adherence friction;
- one highest-leverage correction.

### Monthly

- standardized photos where relevant;
- body-composition and performance trends;
- symptom and intervention review;
- remove interventions that have no measurable value;
- decide the next bounded experiment.

### Quarterly or condition-dependent

- targeted laboratory review where justified;
- dental, vision, or clinical follow-up;
- environmental and product audit;
- research update for active hypotheses.

## Default priority order

1. Safety and diagnosis
2. Sleep
3. Nutrition adequacy and food quality
4. Strength, walking, and aerobic fitness
5. Stress and social regulation
6. Metabolic health and sustainable body composition
7. Oral and digestive health
8. Meaningful exposure reduction
9. Infection or parasite assessment when risk signals exist
10. Targeted topicals, supplements, medications, and procedures

The order changes when a specific disease, deficiency, infection, or urgent condition becomes the actual bottleneck.
